# Monitoring, Evaluation and Learning Framework
## SRHR Learning Myanmar — proposed indicator reference

**Status:** Draft for baseline and donor alignment. Targets are provisional until feasibility, baseline and budget validation.

## 1. Measurement principles

- Measure access and knowledge, not private sexual behavior.
- Do not collect personal health histories or disclosures for routine monitoring.
- Use anonymous/pseudonymous codes only where matching is necessary and safe.
- Keep safeguarding case management separate from M&E datasets.
- Report aggregate results; suppress small cells that could identify participants.
- Disaggregate only where safe, useful and approved.
- Explain missing data, denominator definitions and limitations.

## 3. Baseline and endline

Conduct baseline during Months 1–2 after safe consultation and tool review. Capture relevant knowledge, information access and learning preferences, not sensitive personal experiences. Use the same core questions and scoring rules at endline where appropriate. Document tool changes.

Each report should include number invited/eligible where known and safe, number participating/completing, missing or unmatched responses, safe disaggregation, scores/proportions and limitations. Do not set an arbitrary percentage improvement before baseline and tool quality are known.

## 4. Data collection and quality

- Attendance: non-identifying code, date, activity and ward; keep any re-identification key separately and restrict access if essential.
- Knowledge assessment: no names and no free-text disclosure fields.
- Digital data: aggregate completion and quiz metrics; never export raw session tokens.
- Feedback: anonymous and optional, with no public comment wall.
- Safeguarding: restricted case records held separately by authorized personnel.

M&E focal person checks completeness, duplicate codes, date consistency, definitions and missing data monthly. FR validates digital aggregates; NHE validates field records. Quarterly reviews document decisions and adaptations.

## 5. Learning and accountability

Quarterly reflection: Who is not accessing activities and why? Are age pathways understandable and safe? Are offline options effective? Are referrals current and accessible? Have complaints, incidents or unintended harms emerged? What should be adapted, paused or stopped?

Share findings in accessible, non-identifying formats. Never publish identifiable case stories without appropriate consent and safeguarding review.
